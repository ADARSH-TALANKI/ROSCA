# Gas and cost evaluation

Generated by `npm run bench`. Every figure is measured gas actually consumed on
the EVM, not an estimate.

Solidity 0.8.24, optimizer enabled, 200 runs. Contribution fixed at 1 ETH per
subscriber, so the pot is *groupSize* ETH. Unless stated otherwise the auction
runs at the shipped configuration of **3 bidding rounds** per monthly cycle.

## 3. What the security properties cost

`NaiveChitFund` reconstructs the designs reported in the reviewed literature:
one open plaintext auction, identity by address, unilateral operator custody, no
statutory cap. To compare like with like, `ChitFund` is measured here at **one**
bidding round, so the difference isolates the cost of the security properties

## 4. Scaling ceiling

Three operations iterate the member list, so their gas grows linearly with group
size. Each must fit in a single block, which bounds how large a chit can be.
Against a 30,000,000 gas block limit:

| Operation | Gas at n=5 | Gas at n=50 | Marginal gas per member | Group size at block limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `openBidding` | 56,470 | 269,320 | ~4,730 | ~6,335 |
| `closeBiddingRound` | 141,104 | 296,584 | ~3,455 | ~8,646 |
| `releasePayout` | 160,077 | 1,376,292 | ~27,027 | ~1,109 |

The practical limit is far below the arithmetic one. A real chit fund runs at 10
to 50 subscribers, well inside a single block, so the linear growth is not a
binding constraint at realistic sizes. It would become one for a design that
tried to pool thousands of subscribers in a single contract.

## 5. Monetary cost

Gas is a resource measure. What a cycle actually costs depends on where it is
deployed, which is the point of next semester's platform comparison.

For a 10-subscriber chit at 4,493,301 gas per cycle:

| Deployment | Gas price | Cost per cycle | Cost per member per cycle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ethereum mainnet | 20 gwei | 0.089866 ETH | 0.008987 ETH |
| Ethereum mainnet, congested | 60 gwei | 0.269598 ETH | 0.026960 ETH |
| Layer 2 rollup | 1 gwei | 0.004493 ETH | 0.000449 ETH |
| Permissioned chain (Hyperledger Fabric) | n/a | no gas | no gas |

Multiply the ETH figures by the prevailing ETH price for a fiat number. The ETH
price is deliberately left as a parameter rather than hard-coded, because a
figure printed in a report goes stale immediately.

The last row is the argument for the permissioned deployment. The computation is
identical, the metering is not. A chit fund serving subscribers who save a
thousand rupees a month cannot pass a per-transaction fee on to them, and that
constraint, rather than throughput, is what should decide the platform.

## 6. What price discovery costs

The auction runs several sealed rounds inside one monthly cycle, publishing the
standing lowest bid between rounds so subscribers can respond to it. Sniping is
still impossible inside a round; what changes is that bidders are no longer
guessing blind.

| Group size | Bidding rounds | Gas per cycle | vs 1 round | Winning bid | Discount achieved |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 1 | 1,415,130 | - | 4.2500 ETH | 15.0% |
| 5 | 2 | 1,911,624 | +35% | 4.0000 ETH | 20.0% |
| 5 | 3 | 2,408,118 | +70% | 3.8750 ETH | 22.5% |
| 10 | 1 | 2,424,549 | - | 8.5000 ETH | 15.0% |
| 10 | 2 | 3,458,931 | +43% | 8.0000 ETH | 20.0% |
| 10 | 3 | 4,493,301 | +85% | 7.7500 ETH | 22.5% |

Each additional bidding round costs roughly one more commit-reveal cycle in gas
and buys a deeper discount, because the winner is pushed closer to the statutory
floor. The cost climbs linearly while the discount improves with diminishing
returns, which is the argument for three rounds rather than five.

A deeper discount is not straightforwardly a win. The discount becomes the
dividend, so pushing the winner lower transfers value from whoever needs the pot
most urgently to everyone who can afford to wait. That is precisely the dynamic
the 30% statutory ceiling exists to bound. The bidding round count is a
constructor parameter, so this remains a policy choice per chit rather than one
the contract makes on everybody's behalf.
